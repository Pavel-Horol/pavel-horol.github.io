Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 7, 2026

This policy explains what data the Inkpost browser extension (“the extension”) accesses, why, where it is stored and who receives it. The extension is developed by an independent developer (“we”, “us”). Questions: pashahorolskij4@gmail.com.

Summary

  • Articles are processed locally in your browser. We don’t run any server that receives your data, and we never see it.
  • No analytics, advertising or tracking. We don’t sell or rent data.
  • Data leaves your browser only to do what you asked for: Google sends the email with the book from your Gmail account, your e‑reader’s service (for example Amazon, PocketBook or inkBOOK) receives it at the address you entered and delivers it to your reader, and websites serve the article’s images. For a Kobo, the book goes to your own Google Drive or Dropbox, or by email to your Instapaper. For a Boox or a reMarkable, it goes to your account in the maker’s cloud: Onyx Boox (Onyx and Alibaba Cloud servers) or reMarkable.
  • The extension can send email from your Gmail account. It cannot read your email.

What the extension accesses and why

The page you choose to send

When you click the extension icon or press its keyboard shortcut, the extension reads the page in the current tab: its address, title, author, site name, publication date, language, text and images. It uses them only to build the EPUB book. The extension does not read other tabs or pages you haven’t chosen, and it does not run on the sites you visit.

Article images

To put images in the book, the extension downloads them directly from the websites that host them. For images hosted on other domains it may ask for optional permission to access all websites. It uses this permission only to download article images, and you can refuse it. Some sites serve images only when the request carries the article address, so while downloading, the extension sends that address in the Referer header. These websites receive the same information as when you view the page in your browser: your IP address, the image address and the article address.

Your Google account

When you click Sign in with Google and agree, Google grants the extension two permissions:

  • Send email on your behalf (https://www.googleapis.com/auth/gmail.send). Used only to send the book you chose, or a test message when you click Test send, to the e‑reader address you entered in the settings.
  • Your email address (https://www.googleapis.com/auth/userinfo.email). Used to show which account is connected and as a sign‑in hint, so the extension can renew access without asking you again.

The extension does not ask for and has no access to reading, searching, changing or deleting your email, contacts, files or any other Google data. Google issues it a short‑lived access token, valid for about an hour. The extension has no refresh token and no client secret.

Only if you add a Kobo that gets books through Google Drive and click Connect Google Drive, Google also grants:

  • Access to the files the extension creates (https://www.googleapis.com/auth/drive.file). Used only to create a Rakuten Kobo folder in your Google Drive and upload the books you send into it, where your Kobo picks them up. The extension cannot see, change or delete any other file in your Drive.

Your Dropbox account

Only if you add a Kobo that gets books through Dropbox and click Connect Dropbox, Dropbox grants the extension access to write files and see file names in your Dropbox (files.content.write, files.metadata.read) and to see your account’s email address (account_info.read). The Apps → Rakuten Kobo folder belongs to the Kobo app, so the extension can’t be limited to a folder of its own. It uses the access only to find that folder, upload the books you send into it and show which account is connected. It does not read, change or delete your files. Dropbox issues a short‑lived access token and a refresh token, without any secret.

Your Boox account

Only if you add a Boox: you choose the server of your Boox account and enter its email address, and the extension asks Onyx Boox to email you a one‑time code. With that code Onyx issues a sign‑in token. The extension never asks for or stores a Boox password. It uses the token only to get temporary upload keys, upload the books you send and register them for the BooxDrop app on your Boox. This is an unofficial interface of the Boox service, so the feature is marked experimental.

Your reMarkable account

Only if you add a reMarkable: you get a one‑time code at my.remarkable.com and enter it in the settings, and reMarkable registers the extension as a browser connection of your account and issues a device token. The extension never asks for or stores a reMarkable password. It uses the token only to upload the books you send to your reMarkable library. This is an unofficial interface of the reMarkable cloud, so the feature is marked experimental.

Settings

The names and email addresses of the e‑readers you added, the way a Kobo gets books, and your preferences: image options, the email subject template and whether to add a link to the original.

Everything is stored in your browser on your device. We have no copy.

Who receives data

  • Google, to sign you in, check the token and send the email through the Gmail API. The email contains the EPUB file, the article title as the subject (you can change the template) and a line with the link to the original article. It is sent from your Gmail account, so a copy stays in your Sent folder. Google processes this data under the Google Privacy Policy.
  • Your e‑reader’s service. The email goes to the address you entered for your e‑reader, for example a Send to Kindle address (Amazon), a Send‑to‑PocketBook address (PocketBook) or a Send2inkBOOK address (inkBOOK). That service processes it under its own privacy policy and delivers the book to your reader; Amazon may convert the book to its own format first.
  • Google Drive, if your Kobo gets books through it: the EPUB file is stored in the Rakuten Kobo folder of your Google Drive under the Google Privacy Policy, and your Kobo downloads it from there.
  • Dropbox, if your Kobo gets books through it: the EPUB file is stored in Apps → Rakuten Kobo of your Dropbox under the Dropbox Privacy Policy, and your Kobo downloads it from there.
  • Instapaper, if your Kobo gets articles through it: the email with the book goes to your Instapaper Save via email address and is processed under the Instapaper Privacy Policy.
  • Onyx Boox, if you add a Boox: your Boox account email, to send you the sign‑in code; then the EPUB file, uploaded to Onyx’s cloud storage on Alibaba Cloud (Object Storage Service, in the region of your Boox server: USA, Europe or China) and registered for BooxDrop, which delivers it to your Boox. Onyx processes it under its own privacy policy.
  • reMarkable, if you add a reMarkable: the EPUB file and its title, uploaded to your reMarkable cloud library, from where your reMarkable downloads it. reMarkable processes it under its own privacy policy.
  • Websites hosting the article’s images, as described above.

We don’t share data with anyone else. We don’t sell data, don’t use or transfer it for advertising, and don’t use or transfer it to determine creditworthiness or for lending purposes.

Google API Services User Data Policy

Inkpost’s use and transfer to any other app of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. In particular:

  • data received from Google APIs is used only to provide the features you see in the extension: sending the book you chose, uploading it to the Rakuten Kobo folder in your Google Drive and showing which account is connected;
  • it is transferred to others only as needed for that: the email with the book goes to the address you entered, and a book uploaded to Google Drive stays in your own Drive;
  • it is not used for advertising, including personalized, retargeted or interest‑based advertising;
  • no human reads it: it never reaches us;
  • it is not used to develop, improve or train generalized AI or machine learning models.

The extension’s handling of data also complies with the Chrome Web Store Developer Program Policies, including the Limited Use requirements.

Your choices

  • Click Sign out in the extension’s settings: the token and the account address are deleted from the browser, and the extension asks Google to revoke the token.
  • Revoke the extension’s access at any time in your Google Account.
  • Click Disconnect in a Kobo’s row, switch it to another way of delivery or remove it: the extension deletes its Dropbox tokens and asks Dropbox to revoke them. You can also remove the app in your Dropbox connected apps.
  • Click Disconnect in a Boox or reMarkable row, or remove it: the extension deletes its token from the browser. A reMarkable token stays valid at reMarkable until you remove the browser connection in your account at my.remarkable.com.
  • Remove the extension: the browser deletes all data it stored, settings included.
  • Emails already sent stay in your Gmail Sent folder, and books stay with your e‑reader’s service, such as your Amazon library or PocketBook cloud, or in your Google Drive, Dropbox, Instapaper, BooxDrop or reMarkable library, until you delete them there.

Since we hold no data about you, there is nothing for us to export or delete, but you are welcome to contact us with any question.

Security

All requests go over HTTPS. The Google access token is kept in session memory only and is never written to disk. Dropbox, Boox and reMarkable tokens are kept in local browser storage, so that the reader keeps working after a restart; they are never synced. The extension never asks for the password of any account. The extension contains no client secret and does not load or run code from the internet.

Children

The extension is not directed at children under 13, and we don’t knowingly collect data from them.

Changes to this policy

If the way the extension handles data changes, we will update this page and the date at the top before releasing the new version of the extension.
